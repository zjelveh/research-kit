## Helps
Hovering on or clicking an item shows its details in a pop-up, a small detail view the reader calls up when needed (Interact p. 38). In Munzner's example, a tooltip on one point of an exchange-rate line chart gives its date and the rate of one dollar in euros (Interact p. 38). Our reading: it suits detail the overview does not need, such as an exact date or value.

## Hides
A tooltip does not support overview, so Munzner advises always considering whether the information can be encoded directly in the view (Interact p. 38). Gregor Aisch of the New York Times advises assuming that nobody will see a rollover or tooltip, and making anything important explicit (Interact p. 38). Hover is not available on most touchscreens (Interact p. 16). Our reading: a tooltip shows one item at a time, so it does not help the reader compare items.

## Views
One main view plus a pop-up, which Munzner treats as a specific case of a detail view (Interact p. 38). Our reading: as a detail view, the tooltip shares a subset of the data with the main view, the one item it describes.
